### 2. THE WINNER (Calculated Result)
*   **Selected Formula:** Formula 4: Gelatin_methacrylate (GelMA) & Silk_Fibroin (SF)
*   **One-Sentence Rationale:** Formula 4 provides the most sophisticated balance of tunable mechanical stiffness (matching the arterial wall) and a bioactive protein-based scaffold that promotes rapid endothelialization while suppressing the inflammatory triggers of smooth muscle cell (SMC) hyperplasia.

---

### 3. THE "WHY" (Detailed Logic for the Winner)
*   **Mechanical Fit:** I have utilized a reference range of **0.5 MPa to 1.5 MPa** for the human coronary artery (circumferential Young’s Modulus). Silk Fibroin acts as a structural "reinforcement" within the GelMA matrix. By adjusting the Beta-sheet content of the Silk and the degree of methacrylation in the GelMA, this composite can be precisely tuned to 1.0 MPa, preventing the "compliance mismatch" that typically triggers SMC proliferation.
*   **Swelling Profile:** While GelMA provides a high swelling ratio for drug loading and conformability, pure GelMA can be structurally unstable. The addition of Silk Fibroin provides "interpenetrating network" (IPN) stability. This allows the liner to swell sufficiently to fill the gap between the stent struts and the vessel wall (preventing "dead zones") without the risk of uncontrolled expansion that would occlude the 3.0–3.5mm lumen of a coronary artery.

---

### 4. THE SAFETY AUDIT (Winner's Pros & Cons)
*   **Biological "Green Flags":** 
    *   **Endothelialization:** GelMA contains intrinsic RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) sequences that promote endothelial cell (EC) adhesion. 
    *   **SMC-Inhibition:** The mechanical "softness" (relative to metal) of the SF/GelMA liner reduces the mechanical strain on the media layer of the artery, which is the primary physical trigger for the synthetic SMC phenotype.
    *   **Immune Response:** Silk Fibroin is renowned for its low immunogenicity and has been shown to induce a "pro-healing" M2 macrophage polarization rather than a pro-inflammatory M1 response.
*   **Potential "Red Flags" & Phantom Factors:** 
    *   **Degradation Rate:** The primary risk is the mismatch between the degradation of the hydrogel and the regeneration of the natural internal elastic lamina. If the GelMA degrades too quickly, the Silk "skeleton" must remain hemocompatible.
    *   **Photocrosslinking:** Since GelMA often requires UV/Visible light and a photoinitiator (like LAP), any residual initiator must be strictly washed to prevent localized endothelial toxicity.

---

### 5. REJECTED CANDIDATES (Brief Autopsy)
*   **Rejected due to Toxicity/Inflammation Risk (Score < 4):** 
    *   **Formula 2, 9, 10 (PAM-based):** Polyacrylamide carries the risk of residual acrylamide monomer leaching, which is neurotoxic and highly inflammatory in the bloodstream. These are unsuitable for long-term cardiovascular implantation.
*   **Rejected due to Thrombosis Risk:** 
    *   **Formula 3 & 7 (Chitosan/Chitin):** While bioactive, Chitosan is naturally mucoadhesive and can be pro-thrombotic (hemostatic) if the degree of deacetylation and surface charge is not perfectly controlled. In a high-flow coronary environment, this is a "Safety-First" violation.
*   **Rejected due to Mechanical/Degradation Mismatch:** 
    *   **Formula 8 (Cellulose):** Humans lack cellulase enzymes. A cellulose-based liner would be permanent and non-resorbable, potentially leading to late-stage stent thrombosis or chronic foreign body sensation.
    *   **Formula 6 (Starch):** Starch-based hydrogels often exhibit poor mechanical "toughness" and unpredictable degradation kinetics in the presence of amylase in the blood, risking structural fragmentation (emboli).
*   **The "Runner Up" (Formula 5 - GelMA/PEG):** While PEG is excellent for anti-thrombogenicity, it is "too inert." It fails to actively promote the re-balance of the cellular environment (endothelialization) as effectively as the Silk Fibroin in Formula 4.
